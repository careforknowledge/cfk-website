← All Programmes Model NASS

Civic simulation for young Nigerians.

A deliberative simulation in which participants become delegates to the Model National Assembly of the fictional Federal Republic of Mona — debating real policy questions with genuine consequence.

Visit modelnass.ng
The Problem

Complexity looks simple until you sit inside it.

Many young Nigerians approach governance problems as though they have simple, obvious solutions. This is not ignorance — it is what happens when you have never sat inside a complex policy problem and tried to work through it with people who see it completely differently.

At the same time, young people's perspectives are structurally absent from how policy is made. Model NASS exists to address both problems simultaneously.

The Mechanism

Why the fictional world works.

The Federal Republic of Mona mirrors Nigeria closely enough that the policy questions feel real — but distinctly enough that participants can engage without the weight of personal political allegiance.

When you argue as Hon. Loma Kire of Ugwulore State, you can take seriously a perspective you would normally dismiss. That encounter — genuine engagement with a position you don't hold — is the learning.

How It Works

A full legislative session. Online.

Committee Sessions
Delegates split into committees to deliberate assigned sections of the bill — 70 minutes of genuine debate, recorded in committee position documents.
Plenary Debate
Rapporteurs present committee positions to the full chamber. Cross-committee debate follows — the first time all 72 delegates deliberate together.
Mini Debate
Pre-assigned Lead Speakers argue for and against the bill. The open floor follows — any delegate can speak. The most dynamic 30 minutes of the session.
Three-Way Vote
Delegates vote YES (endorse), NO (oppose), or AMEND (transmit committee proposals). The result is formally transmitted to Mona's Presidency via the Fast-Track Mechanism.
